Your hours come from Hackatime, they're what the [shop](/future/shop) is priced
in, and they're what a reviewer reads alongside your devlogs. A project with
sixty tracked hours and no visible progress gets questions.

## How hours are counted

Hackatime watches your editor and reports which project you're working on. There
is no manual entry and no retroactive fixing, which is why
[getting set up first](/future/getting-started) matters.

Counts:

- Time in your editor on the project you submit — including configs, docs and CAD
  if your tools report.
- Time on the firmware, the site, the schematic — as long as the plugin is
  running and the project name matches.

Doesn't count:

- Anything before the plugin was installed.
- Work on a different project, however related.
- AI-generated work. You get credit for your non-AI hours only.
- Bench time, soldering, and thinking away from the keyboard. Real work,
  uncounted work — which is worth knowing before you plan a hardware build around
  an hours target.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> At most **30%** of the project can be AI-assisted, and there must be no
> AI-generated text or images in the project at all. Even under 30%, something
> that reads as vibecoded can be rejected. See
> [What reviewers check](/future/review).

## Devlogs

A devlog is a short post about what you just did. Write one when you finish
something, not on a schedule:

- **What you built.** One or two sentences.
- **What broke.** The specific thing, and what fixed it.
- **A picture.** The working feature, or the failure if it's more interesting.

"Got the display driving at 60fps — turns out the SPI clock was the problem, not
the framebuffer" is worth ten times "worked on the screen today".

Devlogs also make submission trivial: eight of them and your project description
writes itself. And while your submission sits in the review queue, posting a
devlog is the way to keep going without touching the submission.

## Keeping the two honest

Check your dashboard once a week and ask whether the hours match the devlogs. A
week with hours and nothing shipped usually means you're stuck on something worth
asking about in **#future-ysws** rather than grinding at alone.

If the numbers look wrong — a missing day, hours on the wrong project — raise it
in the channel *when you notice*. It's fixable while it's recent and effectively
not fixable at the end.
