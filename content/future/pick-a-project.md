Pick the future first. It's a constraint, and constraints are what make the
project look like it came from somewhere rather than from a template.

## Which future

| The world | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| **Clean sky** — the one we were promised | Clear air, quiet machines, software that just works. Glossy, calm, running on sunlight. |
| **Neon rain** — cyberpunk, but you own the stack | Rain on chrome, towers above, your rig outrunning theirs. The good ending of a bad timeline. |
| **Reclaimed** — after the collapse, we rebuilt | Salvaged parts, panels on every roof, a network that survived because someone kept patching it. |

You don't have to use one of these three. They're the ones the program is built
around, and picking one gives you a palette, a material and a mood for free.

## What kind of thing

**A tool from that world.** Something that does a real job: an agent that handles
your inbox, a script that runs your room, a device that saves you an hour a week.

**Groundwork for your own future.** A portfolio, a study system, a first product,
a site that gets you hired. Build the thing that gets future-you further along.

**A postcard from there.** Interfaces, holograms, weird hardware, impossible
dashboards. Retro-futurist, Y2K, or straight out of next year — if it looks like
it arrived from a future, it counts.

## Real examples

These come from the program's own idea list, which is worth reading in full on
your program home page:

- **An AI calendar** that manages your inbox and your week — a tool from the
  clean-sky future, roughly 30 hours.
- **A Gamecube JGC-002 controller**, replicated in CAD and printed. An interesting
  piece of industrial design from a past that thought it was the future.
- **Dieter Rams designs**, reproduced or extended — his Braun work is where a lot
  of "futuristic" visual language actually came from. An iPhone dock in that
  language is a legitimate submission.

> [!TIP]
> Ask in **#future-ysws** before you commit a week to an idea. "Does this count as
> futuristic?" gets answered in minutes, and the answer often makes the project
> better rather than smaller.

## Scoping it

Aim for something that works end to end, then make it look like it came from your
future. A working thing with a strong look beats an elaborate thing that doesn't
run — reviewers open it and use it.

Electronics are fully in scope, and they submit slightly differently. See
[Hardware and electronics](/future/hardware).
