Reviewers open your links and work down a list. Most of it is either true or not —
this isn't a taste contest — but two parts do involve judgement, and it's worth
knowing which.

## The checks

**Is it futuristic?** Judgement, and the main one. A project can be well built and
still not be a Future project. Pick a future and commit to it — palette, material,
interface, the copy — and this stops being a risk.

**Does the word "future" appear?** Not judgement. It's there or it isn't.

**Is it open?** Public repo, a `LICENSE` file, a README with images. Hardware also
needs its design files, not just an exported model.

**Can it be opened and used?** A live link, or for hardware the schematics, photos
and build video standing in for one.

**Are the hours tracked?** In Hackatime, linked to the submission.

**Is it yours?** Not a tutorial followed step for step, not someone else's project
recoloured.

## The AI rules, precisely

- **Up to 30% of the project may be AI-assisted.** Past that, it can't be
  submitted.
- **You only get credit for your non-AI hours.** The cap and the hours are
  separate things.
- **No AI-generated text or images in the project at all.** Not the copy, not the
  logo, not the hero image, not the README.
- **Even under 30%**, a project that looks vibecoded — or that plainly didn't take
  real effort — can still be rejected.

The one-line version: build something you're actually proud of.

## If it comes back

You'll get notes naming what failed. Fix that and resubmit; there's no penalty for
resubmitting. If the notes don't make sense, ask in **#future-ysws** — "I don't
understand this note" is a completely reasonable message.

## Moderation

This program is moderated. Abusing the mini-games, submitting plagiarised work, or
otherwise breaking the rules can get your account restricted by an admin. If
something looks wrong on your side, your Profile page has the links for getting
help — that's the fastest route to the people running the program.
