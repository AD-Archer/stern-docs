Do this first. Hours you work before Hackatime is installed can't be counted and
can't be added back, which is the most common way people lose a week of work.

## 1. Sign up for Future

Use the join button in the header. It takes you to the program's welcome page,
where you sign in with your Hack Club account and get added to the round.

If the button isn't there, the round isn't open — the header says so, and the
sidebar explains what happened.

## 2. Join the Slack channel

Everything happens in **#future-ysws**. Ask there rather than in DMs: someone else
has the same question, and answers in the channel become answers for everyone.

New to Hack Club Slack? Sign up at
[hackclub.com/slack](https://hackclub.com/slack) first.

## 3. Install Hackatime

Hackatime counts your hours by watching your editor.

1. Sign in at [hackatime.hackclub.com](https://hackatime.hackclub.com) with your
   Hack Club account.
2. Follow the setup for your editor — VS Code, JetBrains, Neovim and most others
   are supported.
3. Write a few lines, wait a minute, and confirm the time appears on your
   Hackatime dashboard.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Only tracked, non-AI hours count, and hours are what the shop is priced in. An
> afternoon that Hackatime didn't see is an afternoon you can't spend.

## 4. Keep the project in one folder

Hackatime reports a project name, taken from your folder name by default. Keep
your Future work in one folder from the start so the hours line up with the repo
you eventually submit — you'll link those Hackatime projects to your submission
by name.

```bash
# Fine: one folder, one project name.
~/code/future-radio/

# Painful at submission time: three folders Hackatime reports as
# three unrelated projects.
~/code/test2/
~/Desktop/future-final/
~/Downloads/old-thing/
```
