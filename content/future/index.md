Future is a Hack Club YSWS program — *you ship, we ship.*

Everyone had a version of the future in their head as a kid. Clean skies and quiet
machines. Neon rain and a rig that outruns theirs. Something rebuilt out of salvage
after everything went wrong. Future is about building a real thing from whichever
one was yours.

These docs are the practical half: how to get set up, what "futuristic" actually
has to mean for a reviewer, and what has to be true before a submission is
accepted.

> [!NOTE]
> Dates and status live in the header and the sidebar, not in this text. They
> read the program directly, so they're right even when this page is out of date —
> and once the round closes, the join button is gone rather than pointing at
> something you can't enter.

## The two hard rules

Everything else is judgement. These two are not:

1. **It has to be futuristic.** Your future, someone else's, a future that never
   arrived — but the project has to read as *from* a future, not adjacent to one.
2. **It has to include the word "future".** In the name, the interface, the
   README — somewhere a reviewer will see it. Yes, really.

[Submitting your project](/future/submitting) covers what that looks like in
practice.

## The shape of it

1. **Sign up and get your hours counted.** Two accounts and an editor plugin,
   once. See [Getting started](/future/getting-started).
2. **Pick which future, and what kind of thing.** A tool from that world,
   groundwork for your own future, or a postcard from there.
   [Pick a project](/future/pick-a-project) has all three, with real examples.
3. **Build it.** Software, a website, or electronics — all three are in scope, and
   hardware has its own submission path. See
   [Hardware and electronics](/future/hardware).
4. **Ship it.** Open repo, something a reviewer can open, tracked hours.
   [Submitting your project](/future/submitting) is the checklist.

## What you get

Your tracked hours are currency here — the [shop](/future/shop) is priced in
them, and it's stocked with the kind of hardware you'd expect from a program
about futures.
